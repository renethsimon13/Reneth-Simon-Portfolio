Columbia University · Graphics Imaging & Light Measurement Lab · Supervised by Dr. Corey Toler-Franklin
Unifying Multimodal Embedding Spaces for Forensic Evidence Recovery — 1921 Tulsa Race Massacre Investigation
The Problem Historical forensic evidence spans scanned documents, photographs, handwritten texts, and structured graphs, each living in a different representational space. The core challenge is aligning these into a single continuous embedding space where a damaged photograph can be cross-referenced against a document, or a missing detail in one modality inferred from another. This is technically hard because modality distributions are naturally disjoint and naively concatenating embeddings preserves no semantic alignment.

What I Built A multimodal alignment pipeline that projects three distinct embedding types — VAE, CLIP Image, and CLIP Text — into a single unified 512-dimensional space with Gaussian structure. The architecture uses frozen pre-trained encoders followed by learned projection layers, multi-head attention fusion, and a variational head producing well-regularised embeddings. Validated through cross-modal retrieval: text queries retrieve semantically matching images with cosine similarity exceeding 0.98. Trained on Flickr8k across 100 epochs, reducing modality centre distances by over 94%, demonstrating successful alignment. The unified space enables operations previously impossible — directly comparing a text embedding to an image embedding using cosine similarity.

Technical Approach Modality-specific projection networks transform each encoder output into a shared 512-dimensional space. Alignment losses including Fisher loss, centre alignment, and covariance matching enforce distributional overlap, while anti-collapse regularisation prevents variance from collapsing. An 8-head attention fusion mechanism learns content-dependent modality weighting per sample. The variational head applies reparameterisation to produce Gaussian-distributed embeddings suitable for downstream generative tasks. Loss weight tuning was critical: covariance matching at 0.001, anti-collapse at 10.0 for stable training. Cross-modal retrieval uses direct cosine similarity without additional indexing structures.

Stack: PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, OpenAI CLIP, Stable Diffusion VAE, Flickr8k

Manipal University Jaipur — Research Assistant
AI-Enabled Diagnostic X-Ray Analysis for Charcot Neuroarthropathy
The Problem Charcot neuroarthropathy (CN) is a rare but severe complication of diabetes that leads to progressive bone and joint destruction, often resulting in amputation. Despite its severity, awareness among clinicians is limited and there is significant clinical overlap with other conditions such as osteomyelitis, making early and accurate diagnosis difficult. Access to specialist interpretation is not always available in resource-limited settings.

What I Built A multi-stage AI diagnostic pipeline trained on 234 X-rays from patients with and without CN, annotated under the supervision of a senior Foot and Ankle consultant at a tertiary care institute. The IRB-approved, annotated dataset was curated in direct collaboration with 4–5 orthopaedic surgeons; it is not publicly available but can be accessed on request. Classification accuracy: 93.39% on unseen internal test data and 93.3% on an independent external validation set of 15 X-rays. AI predictions showed 91.3% agreement with expert-assigned Brodsky classifications. Segmentation accuracy: 96.85%. Work resulted in a peer-reviewed publication in the Indian Journal of Orthopaedics (2026).

Technical Approach DeepLabV3+ for anatomical region segmentation, EfficientNet-B3 enhanced with ECA attention for multi-class Brodsky classification. ConvLSTM bottlenecks and Attention U-Net encoding preserve anatomical boundaries, while deep supervision improves lesion localisation. The pipeline processes both AP and Lateral X-ray views jointly to reflect the clinical diagnostic workflow.
